THE GIFT OF WORK

We live in a world that is always moving, always rushing, always demanding more. Many of us wake up already feeling behind. Our days are filled with tasks, but we rarely ask where those tasks are taking us.

The Bible begins with a very different picture. Humanity is placed in a garden, entrusted with the responsibility to cultivate it and care for what grows there. Work was not originally a punishment. It was part of the purpose for which we were made.

But somewhere along the way, we began to confuse activity with meaning. We learned to measure our lives by how busy we are, rather than by what our busyness produces. A full calendar is not the same as a fruitful life.

Perhaps the most important question is not how much we can accomplish, but whether the things we accomplish are worth doing. Wisdom begins by choosing what deserves our attention.

A meaningful life is not built by escaping responsibility. It is built by taking responsibility for the things that matter, and doing ordinary work with extraordinary care.
